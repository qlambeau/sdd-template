# ADR-003: Just-In-Time Epic Refinement

## Context

An epic row in a PRD is intentionally high-level. Moving directly from that
row to stories forces epic boundaries, success criteria, and candidate slices
to be decided implicitly during story authoring.

## Decision

Insert an explicit, just-in-time epic refinement stage between PRD approval and
story refinement. `refine-epic` creates one epic brief containing:

- Refined outcome
- In-scope and out-of-scope capability boundaries
- Candidate independently valuable vertical slices
- Success criteria
- Dependencies and open questions

The brief does not author stories or prescribe implementation. Epic identifiers
are scoped to their parent PRD and reuse the identifier from the PRD epic row.

## Alternatives Considered

| Alternative | Reason not selected |
| --- | --- |
| Refine every epic immediately after PRD approval | Creates unnecessary work and causes later epics to be refined against stale context. |
| Keep refinement inside story authoring | Mixes epic and story responsibilities and hides epic-level ambiguity. |
| Use a global epic sequence | Conflicts with the PRD-owned epic hierarchy. |

## Consequences

### Positive

- Epic scope is reviewed before stories are created.
- Story refinement receives an approved candidate-slice list.
- Only the next relevant epic needs to be refined.

### Negative Or Tradeoffs

- There is one additional artifact and promotion step per epic.
- The PRD epic row and brief must be kept traceable.

## Follow-Up Actions

- Use `refine-epic` before `refine-user-stories`.
- Require an approved epic brief for story promotion.
